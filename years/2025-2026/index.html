The magic of positivity

Luis Palacios Vela

In Lie Theory we have 2 perspectives: the complex vs the real, and the algebra vs the geometry. We'll look at two concepts: magical sl_2 triplets, and theta-positivity. The first one lives in the complex algebra, and the latter in the real group. These two classify the same families of semisimple objects. We will be showing examples of both, and attempting to show the relation between these two.

15 May 2026

Multiscale Modeling for Virtual Hair Follicle (Oral Practice)

Rose Johnson-Leiva

Hair follicle is a mini-organ in the skin which undergoes cyclic growth. In recent years it has emerged as a leading system for studying general mechanisms of stem cell biology, tissue growth and regeneration, and cell fate regulation. In this L’Oreal sponsored research project, we develop a Virtual Hair Follicle, dedicated for the study of androgenic alopecia, a common type of hair loss associated with aging. We create a multiscale computational model which integrates numerical PDEs for signaling dynamics and an agent-based submodel for cellular movement and kinetics. We model multiple types of cells and diffusive signaling pathways and are currently working to extend the model into successive hair cycles. This work is in collaboration with my advisor Dr. Qixuan Wang and Dr. Plikus at UC Irvine, and funded by L’Oreal Research Group.

1 May 2026

Uniformly positive Lyapunov exponents with monotone potentials along local unstable leaves

Nick Chiem

Anderson localization is a physical phenomenon that was observed by Phillip Anderson. One definition of localization is when the spectrum of the Schrodinger operator has pure point spectrum with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions. The Lyapunov exponent plays a central role in studying the phenomenon, as uniformly positive Lyapunov exponents paired with a large deviation estimate has been a large indication of localization. Our focus is when one can prove uniform positivity, as Kotani theory would imply that the family of Schrodinger operators has an empty absolutely continuous spectrum. We will discuss the setting and the methods used to show uniform positive Lyapunov exponents for lower Holder potentials along local unstable leaves when generated by hyperbolic dynamics with at least one expanding direction.

24 April 2026

One Thousand And One Arabian Carpets

Jade Leathrum

We investigate modified Sierpiński Carpet fractals, constructed by dividing a square into a uniform nxn grid, removing a subset of the squares at each step, and then repeating that process for each square remaining in that grid. We study these fractals using the fractal zeta functions, first introduced by Michel Lapidus, Goran Radunović, and Darko Žubrinić in their book "Fractal Zeta Functions and Fractal Drums". We give a full classification of all the possible Sierpiński Carpet modifications, and we use that classification to demonstrate algorithms for computing the complex dimensions of every Sierpiński Carpet modification on the nxn grid. This is a practice talk for my Oral Exam, so any feedback would be hugely appreciated.

17 April 2026

Solving PDEs by doing Algebraic Geometry

Christina Grossack

A certain grad student, who I love, talks a lot about how every major theorem in geometry is actually a theorem in PDEs. Wouldn't it be nice to have a major theorem in PDEs that's actually a theorem from geometry? Even better, what about a major theorem in PDEs that's actually a theorem in algebraic geometry! In this talk we'll sketch a proof of the Malgrange–Ehrenpreis Theorem, a major theorem saying that every differential operator with constant coefficients admits a Green's function. The proof will go through the Bernstein-Sato polynomial, whose existence follows from the theory of D-modules in algebraic geometry. In fact, this theory is constructive and has been implemented in algebraic geometry software such as Macaulay2 and Singular. Along the way we'll discuss a pretty relationship between distributional inverses and "normal crossing" singularities in algebraic geometry which puzzled the speaker for at least a year.

A proof of the Hairy Ball theorem via the Stokes theorem

Jiajun Cheng

Exterior calculus does not only allow us to do integration on a manifold, but also yields the magical formula of the Stokes theorem. As an extremely global result, the Stokes Theorem has many applications. For examples: the Cauchy's integral theorem, the Brower's fixed point theorem, and the de Rham theorem. In this talk, we will see a trivial proof of the Hairy Ball Theorem by applying the Stokes Theorem on the unit 2n-spheres, which is not present in a usual textbook (as far as I am aware of). Familiarity of the exterior calculus will not be assumed as I will tell you what differential forms are and list all calculation rules used in the proof.

30 January 2026

Determining numbers of Praeger-Xu graphs

Max Klivans

Let G = (V, E) be a finite simple graph. One might say that G is "very symmetric," if the order of Aut(G) is very large relative to the cardinality of V. Alternatively, we can quantify the symmetry of G with more interesting parameters. A subset S of V is a determining set if the action on V of any automorphism of G is determined by its action on just S. The determining number of G is the minimum cardinality of a determining set of G. The Praeger-Xu graphs PX(n, k) are a family of cool-looking graphs, and in this talk, we compute their determining numbers. If there's time, we'll discuss other interesting symmetry parameters. This talk is based on the paper Determining and distinguishing numbers of Praeger-Xu graphs by Sally Cockburn and myself.

7 November 2025

What happens in the boundary layer?

Ryan Russell

What happens in the boundary layer for solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, asymptotically for small viscosity (high Reynolds number), is not generally known. Heuristic and numerical methods have been proposed and, in special circumstances, verified. I will discuss aspects of the history of this problem, and demonstrate a connection between the work of Chorin (with Marsden and others) in the 1970s, Kato in the 1980s, and Maekawa and others over the past decade or so. This talk is based on joint work with Prof. Jim Kelliher.

17 October 2025

On the hyperkahler nature of the fundamental group

Mo Vaezimonfared

The fundamental group is one of the most basic topological invariants of a space. However, it also encodes a list of flat structures. Hitchin, in his seminal work, by studying a special class of solutions of the self-dual Yang-Mills equations, showed the correspondence between the space of solutions and the moduli space of certain holomorphic/harmonic bundles, called Higgs bundles. On the other side of story, The correspondence between representations and Higgs bundles can be viewed as a Hodge theorem for non-abelian cohomology. This powerful machinery was later extended by Simpson to give a uniformization theorem for kahler manifolds.
